### 📵 I Used a Dumb Phone for a Month — With a Modern Twist

> “Clutter is costly, and its cost is often measured in terms of reduced autonomy.”

> *— Cal Newport,* Digital Minimalism

As a software engineer, technology is central to everything I do. It’s my tool, my craft, and the foundation of how I build and create. But there’s a fine line between using technology intentionally — and being consumed by it.

The infinite scroll, the dopamine-driven app design, the subtle but constant pull to check, swipe, refresh — it wasn’t just a distraction. It was rewiring how I thought and how I spent my time.

I didn’t want to abandon tech — but I *did* want to reclaim control.

So I made a deliberate switch: I replaced my modern smartphone with a “dumb phone.” Not to disconnect entirely, but to reset my relationship with digital tools and reintroduce friction where it mattered.

In a world engineered for distraction, I decided to reclaim my focus — not by throwing away all tech, but by *changing how I used it*. For 30 days, I switched to the **Unihertz Titan Pocket**, a smartphone in a dumbphone’s body. The goal wasn’t full disconnection — I still needed Spotify, navigation, and banking — but I wanted to break the endless scroll loop.

Here’s what I used, how I set it up, the benefits, drawbacks, and whether I’d recommend it.

### 🧠 What Even Is a Dumb Phone?

A **dumb phone** typically refers to:

-   No app store (or heavily limited)
-   No social media
-   No touchscreen or modern UI
-   Just calls, texts, and maybe Snake

*But there’s a spectrum:*

### **Classic Feature Phones**

Nokia 3310, Alcatel Go Flip

Fully off-grid or minimalist users

### **Minimalist Smart Devices**

Light Phone II, Punkt MP02

Designed for peace of mind

### **Locked-Down Smartphones**

Android/iOS with launchers or focus apps

Reduce distraction while keeping power

### **Hybrid Form Factors**

Unihertz Titan Pocket

Retro design, modern software control

### 🔧 Why I Chose the Unihertz Titan Pocket?

Unlike a true dumb phone, the Titan Pocket gave me:

-   **Contactless payments**
-   **Banking apps**
-   **Android Auto for navigation**
-   **Spotify & YouTube (audio only)**
-   **Niagara Launcher** for a minimal, swipe-up app interface

Its small screen and QWERTY keyboard limited mindless usage. I didn’t install video-first apps like Instagram or TikTok. If I ever *did* get the urge to scroll, I opened [**daily.dev**](https://daily.dev/) — a feed of insightful articles, not dopamine-chasing junk.

### Evenings? Just like 2007.

When I needed to check social messages, I visited the **web versions** of platforms. This alone helped distance me from the addictive nature of app-based interaction.

### ✅ The Benefits

### 1\. More Presence

I was *there* — whether it was golfing with friends or just sitting with someone. No mental pull to “just check something.”

### 2\. Less App Fatigue

No pop-ups. No forced updates. No trending nonsense I didn’t ask for.

### 3\. Better Use of Time

By design, the device discouraged passive use. I was intentional. I’d *listen* to music — not scroll. I’d *read* an article — not react to a reel.

### 4\. Awareness of the Invisible Transaction

Modern apps aren’t just tools. They extract attention — and sell it. Using this phone made that painfully obvious.

### ⚠️ The Drawbacks

### 1\. Security Risks

The Titan Pocket runs Android 11, and support has ended. I used Bitdefender, but long-term OS vulnerabilities remain a concern.

### 2\. Form Factor Friction

The small screen made scanning gym barcodes annoying. Some UI elements on apps weren’t optimized for this retro form.

### 3\. Cloud Syncing Issues

My photos were still locked into iCloud. Since the Titan runs Android, accessing or syncing those wasn’t seamless. (I’m solving this in a future post about switching to NAS for storage.)

### 💭 Final Reflection: Would I Recommend It?

**Yes — for anyone who wants a reset without sacrificing modern essentials.**

The Titan Pocket gave me:

-   Modern utility
-   Old-school discipline
-   A clearer head

You don’t need to go full caveman. But you *do* need to be hyperaware of how today’s devices are designed — not for your benefit, but for your attention.

### 📌 TL;DR

-   📱 **Used:** Unihertz Titan Pocket
-   🧠 **Why:** Reduce distraction, retain essentials
-   ✅ **Pro:** Focus, intention, peace
-   ❌ **Con:** Security, screen size, app compatibility
-   👍 **Recommendation:** Yes, especially if you’re feeling digitally burned out
