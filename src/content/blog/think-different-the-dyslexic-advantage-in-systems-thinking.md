*Apple told us to “Think Different.” For dyslexic engineers, thinking differently isn’t a choice. It’s how their brains are wired. And in complex systems work, that difference is becoming a superpower.*

Apple’s old campaign had it right: “*Think Different.*” Albert Einstein, Thomas Edison, Richard Branson, Steve Jobs. All dyslexic. All systems thinkers who changed the world.

What if thinking differently isn’t just a marketing slogan but an actual cognitive advantage?

### The Pattern Spotters

![](https://cdn-images-1.medium.com/max/643/1*mvp20zTY47V6i1_xy0QsIg.png)

Working in software systems, I’ve noticed something curious. When it breaks, you may find that one engineer who spots the pattern everyone else misses. An issue in service A connects to latency in service B, which links to database exhaustion from that deployment three days ago.

That’s not luck, that’s a different intelligence.

### The Science Behind It

Research from Cambridge University is rewriting what we know about dyslexia. Dr. Helen Taylor’s team found that dyslexic brains aren’t broken. They are specialised to explore the unknown. Optimised for pattern recognition across complex systems.

Brain imaging shows these advantages come from fundamental neural differences present from birth.

### Why Complex Systems Need Different Thinking

Traditional analysis breaks problems into parts. Dyslexic cognition naturally grasps how parts interact as wholes. Researchers call it “*global exploratory learning.*” Understanding complexity through interconnections rather than components.

![](https://cdn-images-1.medium.com/max/620/1*sm0J3YAfzTKwKYzeBCV0KA.png)

Dyslexic engineers often excel at:

-   Spotting system-wide patterns in data
-   Designing resilient architectures
-   Reducing Noise & Improving Signal rate
-   Connecting seemingly unrelated incidents

### The Entrepreneur Connection

A 2003 study found that **40% of successful entrepreneurs are dyslexic**. That’s four times the general population rate.

Richard Branson: “*My dyslexia became my massive advantage. It helped me think creatively and laterally, and see solutions where others saw problems.*”

![](https://cdn-images-1.medium.com/max/860/1*r3taULLFRn7soamqoVJd3w.png)

Entrepreneurship is systems design. Identifying market gaps, orchestrating resources, creating value networks. The same cognitive traits that make reading difficult make systems thinking natural.

### Building Better Teams

![](https://cdn-images-1.medium.com/max/660/1*3yB-dSfL1-Iar5XbzQW8vA.png)

The best engineering teams combine different cognitive styles. Detail-oriented linear thinkers ensure correct implementation. Big-picture systems thinkers ensure architectural coherence.

Cognitive diversity reduces single points of failure in problem-solving.

### Changing the Story

![](https://cdn-images-1.medium.com/max/730/1*fa4tZef3aH78Ej9J5BpuwQ.png)

We’ve spent decades focused on what dyslexic people can’t do. Reading difficulties. Spelling problems. Processing delays.

Researchers now advocate for “*shifting focus to dyslexic advantages.*” In technology, this matters. Instead of seeing dyslexia as something to overcome, we can recognise it as cognitive specialisation suited to complex systems work.

### The Hidden Architecture

Every complex system has hidden architecture. Patterns and dependencies that determine how it behaves under stress. Understanding this requires the kind of global, pattern-oriented thinking that dyslexic cognition provides.

The engineers who excel at this level often share certain traits: they see connections others miss, think in visual terms, focus on interactions rather than components.

These aren’t accidents. They’re enhanced abilities vital for adapting to changing environments.

### Why This Matters Now

As systems become increasingly complex (distributed cloud, AI systems, global supply chains), dyslexic thinking becomes more valuable, not less.

The question isn’t whether dyslexic individuals can succeed in business & technology. The evidence shows they do. The question is whether organisations will leverage it.

Learning of my own **dyslexia** not only liberated me from comparison but enabled me to **work in a way that matters to me**.

Think **Different**
